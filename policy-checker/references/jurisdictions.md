# Jurisdiction Quick Reference

Use this reference during drafting (Phase 4) to add jurisdiction-specific
clauses. Always confirm the user's jurisdiction in the interview first — never
assume. When multiple jurisdictions apply, draft one Privacy Policy with
jurisdiction-specific sections (e.g., "Additional rights for California
residents") and one set of terms with the broadest compliant standard.

## GDPR (EU/EEA + UK GDPR)

- Legal bases: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public
  task, legitimate interests — name the basis per processing purpose.
- Data subject rights: access, rectification, erasure, restriction,
  portability, objection, withdrawal of consent.
- Art. 22: right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal or
  similarly significant effects (includes human review request).
- Transfers outside EU: adequacy decision, SCCs, or DPF (US) — must state the
  mechanism.
- Breach notification: 72h to supervisory authority.
- Cookies: consent required before non-essential cookies (ePrivacy); consent
  must be as easy to withdraw as to give.
- Supervisory authority: e.g., AEPD (Spain), CNIL (France), ICO (UK).
- Data minimization and storage limitation: every data item needs purpose +
  retention period.

## CCPA/CPRA (California)

- Applies when: for-profit business meeting thresholds (revenue > $25M, or
  100k+ consumers' data, or 50%+ revenue from selling/sharing data) serving
  California residents.
- Rights: know, delete, correct, opt out of sale/sharing, limit use of
  sensitive personal info, non-discrimination.
- "Sale/share" includes ad-tech pixel data sharing — many analytics and ad
  SDKs count.
- Required disclosures: categories of data collected, purposes, retention
  periods, third-party sharing.
- Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information link if sale/sharing occurs.

## COPPA (US, children under 13)

- Applies to apps knowingly (or actually) directed at children under 13.
- Requires verifiable parental consent for collecting children's data,
  privacy notice for parents, data minimization, and strict ad limits.

## PIPEDA (Canada)

- Consent-based (meaningful consent), purposes must be identified, right to
  access and correct, accountability principle.

## LGPD (Brazil)

- Similar structure to GDPR: legal bases, rights, DPO ("encarregado")
  recommended, ANPD as authority.

## Spain-specific

- Legal Notice (Aviso Legal) mandatory for websites (LSSI).
- Marketing emails require unsubscribe in every message (LSSI).
- AEPD is the supervisory authority; Spanish-language documents recommended.

## US federal extras

- CAN-SPAM: physical address + unsubscribe in commercial emails.
- TCPA: prior express consent for marketing SMS (and autodialed calls).
- Section 230 (user content platforms): still draft takedown/DMCA process.

## Mobile store requirements

- Apple App Store: ATT prompt if tracking across apps; in-app account
  deletion when account creation exists; privacy "nutrition labels" must match
  actual data practices; subscription terms must disclose auto-renewal.
- Google Play: Data safety form must match actual practices; account deletion
  required; target audience and content rating declarations.

## AI-specific

- EU AI Act: transparency obligations for AI systems interacting with users;
  disclose AI-generated content where applicable.
- Art. 22 GDPR for automated decision-making; state human review availability.
- Many US states now have AI disclosure rules — use placeholders if
  jurisdiction unclear.

## When jurisdiction is unknown

- Ask again in the interview. If the user genuinely does not know, default to
  a base Privacy Policy with placeholders and add jurisdiction sections once
  known. Never guess the governing law.
